From need to done in minutes.

Why we're starting this

I'm Tracy, a home health nurse in Washington State. Home health puts you in people's bathrooms and kitchens. You see the tub someone can't safely get into and the shoes that don't fit swollen feet. Often the fix costs less than $100, and nobody can pay for it quickly.

For two years I relied on an agency foundation's patient-assistance fund, about $200 per patient per year, and I was one of its heaviest users. Need was a checkbox, so I could act the same day I saw the problem. I used it for shower chairs and grab bars so people could bathe safely again, supportive shoes and compression garments for swollen feet and legs, and grabbers so someone could reach things on their own. I also bought talking scales and large-print reminder clocks, pill organizers to keep medications straight, and fall-alert devices for people who live alone.

Each one cost very little, and each one changed someone's day at home.

Then the rules changed. The fund began requiring proof of need: a long form, plus bank statements or pay stubs to prove income. I stopped using it. I won't ask a patient to hand over their bank statements for a pair of shoes.

Even when it worked, it was slow. Approvals took 24 to 48 hours. Clinicians covered costs on their personal cards and waited about a month for a paper reimbursement check.

So I'm starting the Clinician Microgrant Fund. I'm on leave from nursing until mid-December and putting that time into getting it off the ground, including building our two apps, GrantDirect and the MedShed Kit.

Clinicians already know what their patients need. The Clinician Microgrant Fund trusts that judgment and gets out of the way.

How it works

  1. Request in 60 seconds

    The clinician sends a quick request from a product link in our app, GrantDirect.

  2. Get the green light

    GrantDirect's AI approves items within our guidelines instantly. Anything else gets a human review within 24 hours.

  3. Deliver and teach

    The clinician gets the item to the patient and shows them how to use it safely.

  4. Get reimbursed fast

    Reimbursement goes straight to the clinician's bank account. No paper checks.

  5. Follow up

    We check in with the patient or family to make sure they understand and send a short teaching video for their item, made by clinicians. About six weeks later, we ask if it made a difference and invite them to share their story.

What a microgrant can do

Everyday items that insurance is too slow for or won't cover, chosen by the clinician who sees the need in the home:

Safe bathing

A shower chair, tub bench or handheld shower, so someone can bathe at home again without fear of falling.

Getting around

Supportive shoes that fit swollen feet, or a walker narrow enough for the bathroom door.

Staying independent

A reacher, sock aid or raised toilet seat that lets someone do things for themselves again.

Managing at home

A pill organizer, talking scale or large-print clock that makes daily care easier to follow.

Reuse first: we check community supply closets before buying anything new. That's why we're building the MedShed Kit.

Privacy and trust: we don't collect diagnoses, and follow-up texts to patients never mention health information. A share of requests are randomly reviewed to keep the fund honest.

AI-powered from day one

Built to run on AI, with people in charge

Most of the delay in patient assistance is paperwork. We're designing the Clinician Microgrant Fund so AI handles the paperwork for everyone involved, and people make the judgment calls.

For clinicians

GrantDirect's AI checks each request against our guidelines and approves it in seconds. Anything unusual goes to a person.

For donors

AI-assisted bookkeeping tracks every dollar, so donors can see clear, up-to-date reports on where their money went. With their permission, patients can share their stories here and on our YouTube channel, in writing or on video.

For compliance

It starts with a simple text to the patient: Did you get it? Did your clinician show you how to use it? Any questions? Exchanges and returns are handled in the app, every step is logged, and random audits keep everyone honest.

For the organization

We use AI tools to set up and run the nonprofit itself, keeping overhead low enough to pay for without donor dollars.

Our goal: 100% of donor dollars go to patients

We plan to cover overhead like website hosting and AI fees from other sources: clinician membership dues (with continuing education for members), our YouTube channel, and affiliate programs. That way, every donated dollar can go to patient needs.

Building first

The MedShed Kit

Shower chairs, walkers and commodes sit unused in garages while a neighbor down the road goes without. A MedShed is a community closet that gets that equipment back into use.

We're building a low-cost app and starter kit so any church, senior center or neighborhood group can run one, with AI photo intake and QR labels so clinicians can see what's on the shelf nearby.
